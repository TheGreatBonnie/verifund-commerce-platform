# What Are Negotiation Skills?

Negotiation skills are the abilities a person uses to communicate, influence decisions, resolve disagreements, exchange proposals, and reach mutually acceptable agreements.

Negotiation happens whenever two or more parties have different interests, expectations, or objectives but need to find common ground.

For example:

- A buyer wants to purchase a laptop at the lowest possible price.
- A seller wants to sell the laptop at a profitable price.
- Both parties negotiate until they reach an agreement that satisfies their important requirements.

Negotiation is not simply about convincing someone to accept your proposal. It is about understanding what each party wants, identifying opportunities for compromise, and reaching an agreement that creates value for both sides.

## 2. How negotiation works in practice

A typical negotiation follows a sequence of stages.

1. Preparation

   Understand your objectives, priorities, constraints, and alternatives.

2. Opening proposal

   One party presents an initial offer or request.

3. Discussion and clarification

   Both parties explain their positions, ask questions, and identify their interests.

4. Counteroffers

   Each party evaluates the other's proposal and suggests adjustments.

5. Bargaining and trade-offs

   The parties explore concessions and alternative arrangements.

6. Agreement or rejection

   They accept the proposed terms, continue negotiating, or end negotiations.

7. Documentation

   They record the final agreed terms and responsibilities.

## 3. Understanding negotiation strategies

Negotiators can use different strategies depending on their objectives.

| Strategy                  | Explanation                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Competitive negotiation   | Focuses on obtaining the most favorable outcome for your side.                                                     |
| Collaborative negotiation | Focuses on creating value and benefiting both parties.                                                             |
| Compromise                | Both parties make concessions to reach an acceptable middle ground.                                                |
| Value-based negotiation   | Uses quality, benefits, evidence, and overall value rather than focusing only on price.                            |
| Principled negotiation    | Separates people from the problem and focuses on interests, objective criteria, and mutually beneficial solutions. |

An effective negotiator knows when to use each approach.

# Translating negotiation skills into AI agent capabilities in an A2A protocol

The key idea is to transform each human negotiation skill into a specific capability that an AI agent can execute, expose, and communicate through the Agent2Agent (A2A) protocol.

In Verifund, the Buyer Agent and Seller Agent should not simply exchange messages generated by an LLM. They should behave like autonomous commercial representatives: understand their owner's interests, evaluate proposals, negotiate within authorized limits, and produce structured agreements.

A useful way to think about this is:

- Negotiation skill: What a capable human negotiator knows how to do.
- AI agent capability: What the agent must be able to reason about or execute.
- A2A interaction: How the agent communicates that capability and its results to another agent.
- Verifund enforcement: How the platform validates that the resulting agreement is authorized and safe.

Let's break this down.

## 1. Map human negotiation skills to AI capabilities

The following table translates conventional negotiation skills into capabilities that can be implemented in the Buyer Agent and Seller Agent.

| Human negotiation skill  | AI agent capability                       | What the agent does                                                                |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Active listening         | Intent and requirement extraction         | Understands the other agent's offer, conditions, constraints, and priorities.      |
| Preparation and research | Context gathering and market analysis     | Retrieves product information, pricing, inventory, and market alternatives.        |
| Communication            | Structured negotiation messaging          | Produces clear offers, counteroffers, questions, and explanations.                 |
| Persuasion               | Value-based proposal generation           | Explains why a proposal offers value to the other party.                           |
| Problem-solving          | Constraint and trade-off optimization     | Finds alternative combinations of price, delivery, warranty, and other terms.      |
| Emotional intelligence   | Interaction and sentiment interpretation  | Detects dissatisfaction, urgency, hesitation, and potential negotiation deadlocks. |
| Compromise               | Concession management                     | Determines which terms can be adjusted without violating its owner's interests.    |
| Decision-making          | Offer evaluation and acceptance reasoning | Decides whether to accept, reject, counter, or escalate a proposal.                |

These are not necessarily separate agents. They are capabilities that can be implemented as tools, subagents, or nodes inside each agent's workflow.

## 2. The eight core negotiation capabilities

### Capability 1: Intent and requirement understanding

Buyer Agent + Seller Agent

A human negotiator listens to understand what the other party actually wants rather than reacting only to the words being spoken.

An AI agent needs the same ability.

For example, a buyer might tell their agent:

The Buyer Agent should extract structured requirements:

The Seller Agent must perform a similar process when interpreting the buyer's requirements.

Implementation capabilities:

- Natural-language understanding.
- Intent classification.
- Entity extraction.
- Requirement prioritization.
- Constraint identification.
- Negotiation context management.

A2A interaction: The agents exchange structured requirement and proposal information instead of relying entirely on unstructured conversation.

### Capability 2: Negotiation preparation and research

Buyer Agent + Seller Agent

A good negotiator prepares before making an offer.

The Buyer Agent should gather information that helps it determine whether a seller's offer is reasonable.

For example:

- Product market price.
- Prices from competing sellers.
- Product condition.
- Seller reputation.
- Shipping costs.
- Warranty terms.
- Available alternatives.

The Seller Agent prepares using different information:

- Current inventory.
- Product acquisition cost.
- Authorized selling price.
- Minimum acceptable selling price.
- Shipping costs.
- Fulfillment capacity.
- Available promotional discounts.

The agents then have the context necessary to negotiate intelligently.

Implementation capabilities:

- Product research.
- Market-price analysis.
- Seller and buyer context retrieval.
- Inventory verification.
- Competitive comparison.
- Negotiation strategy generation.

An important distinction: the Buyer Agent should not access confidential seller pricing information, and the Seller Agent should not access the buyer's private budget unless the buyer chooses to disclose it.

### Capability 3: Offer generation and communication

Buyer Agent + Seller Agent

A human negotiator knows how to make a proposal that communicates what they want and what they are willing to exchange.

Your agents need an offer-generation capability.

The Seller Agent receives the proposal and evaluates it against its commercial policies.

The important capability is not simply generating an attractive message. The agent must generate a proposal containing valid, explicit, machine-readable commercial terms.

Implementation capabilities:

- Offer construction.
- Structured payload generation.
- Commercial term validation.
- Proposal explanation.
- Offer versioning.
- Offer transmission.

### Capability 5: Trade-off optimization

Buyer Agent + Seller Agent

This capability allows agents to negotiate beyond price.

A human negotiator understands that a deal can become more attractive by adjusting several terms.

For example, a seller may not be able to reduce the product price but may be able to offer:

- Free shipping.
- An extended warranty.
- Faster delivery.
- Additional accessories.
- A different product configuration.

The Buyer Agent can evaluate these alternatives based on the buyer's priorities.

Consider the following:

| Negotiation term | Buyer's preference | Seller's position       |
| ---------------- | ------------------ | ----------------------- |
| Product price    | Lower              | Limited discount        |
| Shipping         | Free               | Can offer free shipping |
| Warranty         | 6 months           | Can offer 12 months     |
| Delivery         | 3 days             | Can deliver in 2 days   |

The agents can find an agreement that provides value without requiring the seller to make an unacceptable price reduction.

Implementation capabilities:

- Multi-objective optimization.
- Preference weighting.
- Concession analysis.
- Alternative proposal generation.
- Utility scoring.
- Pareto-efficient agreement identification.

This is where your agentic commerce system becomes more sophisticated than a simple automated price-negotiation chatbot.

### Capability 6: Concession management

Buyer Agent + Seller Agent

A concession is something a negotiator is willing to give up to reach an agreement.

Your agents need to understand:

1. Which terms are negotiable.
2. Which terms are non-negotiable.
3. How much flexibility they have.
4. What they should request in exchange for a concession.

For example:

The Seller Agent might be authorized to:

- Reduce the product price by up to $100.
- Offer free shipping.
- Extend the warranty by six months.

However, it cannot sell below its authorized minimum price.

The Buyer Agent might be authorized to:

- Increase its offer by up to $50.
- Accept paid shipping.
- Consider a shorter warranty.

But it cannot exceed the buyer's maximum authorized total.

The agents should use concession strategies rather than randomly changing offer amounts.

Implementation capabilities:

- Concession planning.
- Negotiation boundary management.
- Conditional concession generation.
- Reciprocal concession reasoning.
- Mandate validation.

Crucially, the LLM should not be the authority that decides whether a concession is permitted. A deterministic policy service should enforce the actual limits.

### Capability 7: Negotiation deadlock resolution

Buyer Agent + Seller Agent

Sometimes two agents cannot agree.

For example:

- Buyer maximum: $1,400.
- Seller minimum: $1,450.

The agents need to recognize that their current negotiation positions do not overlap.

Instead of repeatedly exchanging similar counteroffers, they should explore alternatives:

- Reduce shipping costs.
- Change the warranty.
- Offer a different product configuration.
- Propose a later delivery date.
- Request human intervention.

Implementation capabilities:

- Deadlock detection.
- Negotiation progress analysis.
- Alternative solution generation.
- Escalation management.
- Negotiation termination.

This prevents agents from entering endless counteroffer loops.

### Capability 8: Agreement evaluation and closing

Buyer Agent + Seller Agent + Verifund

Human negotiators must know when an agreement has been reached and ensure both parties understand the final terms.

Your agents need an agreement-closing capability.

When both agents agree, the final terms should be consolidated into a structured agreement.

For example:

```
{
  "agreement_id": "agr-001",
  "product_id": "macbook-pro-001",
  "quantity": 1,
  "unit_price": 1370,
  "shipping_cost": 30,
  "warranty_months": 6,
  "total_amount": 1400,
  "currency": "USD",
  "status": "pending_buyer_approval"
}
```

The agents should verify that:

- Both parties agreed to the same terms.
- The offer version is current.
- The agreement satisfies their mandates.
- All conditions are explicit.
- The agreement has not expired.

Verifund then becomes responsible for authoritative agreement creation, buyer approval, payment authorization, fulfillment verification, and settlement.

An agent's acceptance is not equivalent to payment authorization.

These are Verifund-defined business event types, not built-in A2A protocol message types. They can be carried through A2A messages using structured data and agreed payload schemas.

### C. A2A tasks: manage longer negotiations

A negotiation may require multiple exchanges.

For example:

1. Buyer Agent proposes an offer.
2. Seller Agent evaluates the offer.
3. Seller Agent submits a counteroffer.
4. Buyer Agent evaluates the counteroffer.
5. Seller Agent proposes a trade-off.
6. Buyer Agent accepts the revised terms.

This should be represented as a durable negotiation task, with the task identifier correlating the exchanges.

The task can remain active while the agents negotiate asynchronously.

### D. A2A artifacts: preserve negotiation results

Artifacts should contain structured outputs that other systems can consume.

For example:

- Initial buyer offer.
- Seller counteroffer.
- Negotiation history.
- Final agreed terms.
- Agreement proposal.

Verifund can consume the final artifact, validate its terms, and create the authoritative agreement record.

## 6. Separate LLM reasoning from deterministic capabilities

This distinction is particularly important for a commerce platform handling real payments.

Not every negotiation capability should be controlled by an LLM.

| Capability                    | Recommended implementation             |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Understand buyer requirements | LLM                                    |
| Analyze seller proposal       | LLM + structured validation            |
| Generate counteroffers        | LLM + policy constraints               |
| Compare product alternatives  | Search and ranking algorithms          |
| Calculate total price         | Deterministic calculation              |
| Enforce buyer maximum budget  | Deterministic policy engine            |
| Enforce seller minimum price  | Deterministic policy engine            |
| Detect expired offers         | Deterministic workflow logic           |
| Validate agreement versions   | Deterministic agreement service        |
| Authorize payment             | Verifund approval and payment services |

The LLM can recommend an action, but deterministic services must determine whether that action is permitted.

For example, if the buyer has authorized a maximum of $1,400, the Buyer Agent must not be able to submit a $1,450 offer simply because its LLM considers the deal attractive.

Likewise, accepting a seller's counteroffer must not automatically authorize payment.

| 4        | Counteroffer reasoning            | Enables genuine agent-to-agent negotiation.                       |
| 5        | Mandate and concession management | Keeps negotiations within authorized limits.                      |
| 6        | Trade-off optimization            | Enables negotiations beyond price.                                |
| 7        | Deadlock resolution               | Prevents endless negotiation loops.                               |
| 8        | Agreement generation              | Converts negotiation outcomes into enforceable transaction terms. |

My architectural recommendation: Build a reusable `Negotiation Engine` inside both the Buyer Agent and Seller Agent. Give it shared concepts such as `NegotiationContext`, `NegotiationStrategy`, `OfferEvaluator`, `CounterOfferGenerator`, `ConcessionManager`, and `AgreementEvaluator`. Each agent supplies its own mandate, private context, and commercial policies.

A2A then provides the communication between those independent engines, while Verifund remains the trusted transaction coordinator.

The most important design principle is that agents should negotiate autonomously, but they should never have unrestricted authority to commit their owners to a financial transaction.&#x20;

A useful next step would be to design the actual `Negotiation Engine` interfaces, state schemas, A2A message payloads, and LangGraph workflow for both agents.
